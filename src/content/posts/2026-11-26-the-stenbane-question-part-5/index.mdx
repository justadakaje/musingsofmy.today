*The Stenbane Question is a five-part story about a family of WoW: Forever alts. Eight Stenbanes told eight stories about where their name came from at the Everlook inn: a mountain ([Part 1](/2026/11/12/the-stenbane-question-part-1/)), a candle and an acronym ([Part 2](/2026/11/16/the-stenbane-question-part-2/)), a gravestone and a registry form ([Part 3](/2026/11/19/the-stenbane-question-part-3/)), a kodo and a dream ([Part 4](/2026/11/23/the-stenbane-question-part-4/)). One Stenbane said nothing. Then he left.*

## Part 5: Two Names, One Metal

Winterspring before dawn is the quietest place in the world.

The snow swallows sound. The Frostsabers are asleep, or pretending to be. Down in the valley, the furbolgs of Winterfall have stopped drumming for the night, and the only thing moving in the blue dark is the wind coming down off the peaks, and one dwarf, walking into it.

Tung had stopped at the inn's kitchen on the way out, because he was not an animal. He had a tin cup of coffee in one hand, black and bitter, and his shield on his back, its straps checked twice by lantern light. He had done that every morning for as long as he could remember. Coffee. Straps. Boots. Then the day could start, and whatever it threw at him, he would at least meet it caffeinated and properly buckled.

He had the compass out.

Nobody in the family had ever seen him read it, and there was a reason for that: it didn't point north. It had never pointed north. The old cracked needle swung lazily under its glass and settled, always, on stone, and not just any stone. It pointed at the stone that mattered. Tung had never figured out how it decided. He had simply learned to follow it.

This morning it pointed east, toward the ridges above the Ice Thistle Hills.

He followed it, and through the soles of his boots, faint and far off, he felt the tremor again.

---

He found Wulf on a ledge above the snowline, sitting cross-legged in front of a Rich Thorium Vein.

The Tauren had built a small fire in a ring of stones and set a dented camp pot on it, and the pot was steaming. Fram was curled in the snow beside him with his nose under his tail, one eye open. Wulf didn't look around when Tung came up the slope. He simply reached into his pack, took out a second tin cup, filled it from the pot, and held it out.

Tung swapped his half-empty cup for it.

It was coffee, and it was worse than Vizzie's. Darker. Bitterer. Boiled, probably, since midnight.

Tung took a long swallow and nodded, the highest praise he knew how to give.

They sat. The sky over the peaks went from black to dark blue. Fram closed his open eye.

---

"You didn't tell a story," said Tung eventually.

"No," said Wulf.

It was the first word he'd said in two days. His voice was very deep and a little rough, like a door that didn't get opened much.

"Everybody else did."

"Everybody else *talked*," said Wulf.

Tung acknowledged this was true.

The Tauren looked at the compass in Tung's hand for a long moment. Then he said, in the same slow, careful Common Thun used, but with half the words, as though every one cost him a copper:

"That was my uncle's."

---

The fire cracked. Down in the valley, a yeti called and another answered.

"Krajed," said Wulf. "Shaman. Long before Thun. He walked the cracks in the world, where the land splits. He put his ear to them, and he listened." He was quiet for a while. "He said the earth was getting ready to break. Not soon. Someday. He said that when it did, totems would burn."

Tung said nothing. His hand had closed around the compass.

"He carried a stone for years," said Wulf. "Fist-sized. Dark. So heavy you'd think it was bigger. He said it came from the deepest crack he ever found, and it would not melt in any fire. *Wolfram*, he called it." Wulf looked at the vein beside them, then back at the compass. "One year he came home without it. Without the stone, and without his compass. He said he gave them both to a dwarf."

"Where," said Tung. It wasn't really a question.

"A tunnel. Falling in. Fire and poison behind it." Wulf's ears flattened slightly. "Small ones running out. And a dwarf at the mouth of it, holding up the roof with his shield and his back, so the small ones could get out."

---

Tung remembered.

He remembered Dun Morogh, the day Gnomeregan fell. The air thick and green and wrong. Gnomes pouring out of the dark in their hundreds, singed and coughing, carrying what they could. A side passage going, the timbers screaming, and a young dwarf smith with more stubbornness than sense putting his shoulder and his shield under the lintel and refusing to let it come down until the last of them were out.

And he remembered, in all that smoke and noise, a figure that made no sense at all: a Tauren, an impossible distance from Mulgore, there under a truce that no banner had signed, standing at his side in the dust. He hadn't held up the roof. He'd laid his huge hand flat on the rock, and the rock had stopped shaking, just long enough.

Afterward, when the passage was empty and finally allowed to fall, the Tauren had pressed something into Tung's hands. A fist-sized lump of ore, dark and dull and so heavy it nearly pulled his arms down. A cracked leather compass, still warm.

"*The spirits are screaming, brother*," the Tauren had said, in broken Common. "*Totems will burn in the fire to come. Forge something that will not melt.*"

Then he was gone.

Tung had carried the ore to the Great Forge of Ironforge, and worked it for a month, and the forge had complained the whole time, because the stuff just *would not melt*. And when he was done, he'd set it as the boss of his shield, the round heavy center where every blow lands.

He reached back now and tapped it with a knuckle. It made the same dull, solid sound it always made.

It hadn't melted. Not once. Not in anything.

---

"He named me after that stone," said Wulf. "Wolfram. Too big for my mouth." A pause. "Wulf."

Tung looked at the shield. He looked at the Tauren.

"Tungsten," he said. "Too big for mine. Tung."

Wulf nodded slowly.

"Same stone," said Wulf.

"Same stone," said Tung.

Two names for one metal, the hardest there is: one name for each side of a world that was always arguing over which name was the right one. A dwarf and a Tauren. Alliance and Horde. Sitting on the same rock, drinking the same terrible coffee.

---

"I remember the dwarf holding the ceiling."

They both turned. Ting was standing at the top of the slope, wrapped in her purple robe and somebody's spare blanket, nose red with cold. She had followed Tung out of the inn. She had always been a light sleeper. People who've had to run from a city in the dark usually are.

"I was very small," she said, picking her way down to the fire. "And everything was on fire, and I got stuck, and somebody very big picked me up and put me on his shoulder and told me to hold on." She sat down next to Tung, close enough to share the warmth. "So I held on to his beard."

"You pulled out a braid," said Tung.

"I know." She opened her small fist. In it, tied with a bit of faded red thread, was a short braid of coarse black dwarven beard, singed at one end. "I kept it."

For a long moment, nobody said anything at all.

Wulf poured her a cup of coffee. Ting took one sip, made a face like she had been personally betrayed, and kept holding it for the warmth.

---

The sky had gone pale gold over the eastern peaks. Tung looked into the fire. Then he looked at Wulf.

"So," he said. "Is *that* where Stenbane comes from?"

Wulf considered the question for a very long time. Long enough for the fire to settle. Long enough for Fram to stretch, yawn and resettle in a new patch of snow. Long enough that Ting started to lean forward.

"No idea," said Wulf.

---

The tremor came back. Not faint this time.

Fram was on his feet and growling before anyone else had noticed. Then the snow at the top of the ridge broke apart, and an Ice Thistle yeti came over it at a full run, roughly the size of the Everlook inn and twice as angry about being awake.

Tung was up before he'd decided to stand, shield off his back and on his arm in one motion. He didn't even put his coffee down. He measured the slope, the drop, the angle of the charge, and the fact that there were two people behind him who were not in plate, and he planted his feet and did the arithmetic every Protection Paladin does in the half second before the pull. *Blast radius. Nobody goes past me.*

"*RICH THORIUM VEIN!*" shouted a voice from the other side of the ridge, and a large teal-haired cheetah came pelting around the rocks in Travel Form, saw the yeti, skidded in the snow, and shifted back into a night elf mid-skid.

"Oh," said Thor. "Yeti."

"*Did you pull that?*"

"I may have pulled *something*."

---

What happened next took about forty seconds and has been retold at every family gathering since, and the version is never the same twice.

Tong came over the ridge at a dead sprint, yelling, and Charged the yeti in the knees. "*Charge has no cooldown in my HEART!*" The yeti fell over him, which was more or less the plan. Penny was right behind him, and a shimmering Power Word: Shield bloomed around Tong a split second before the yeti landed on him, then another around Ting, then around Tung, who didn't need one and got one anyway.

"You looked tired," Penny said.

Kobalt summoned a Voidwalker, which loomed up out of the snow, looked at the yeti, and visibly reconsidered its contract. The imp hurled firebolts from the safety of Kobalt's hood. Ting set down her coffee, raised both hands and Frost Nova'd the yeti's feet to the ground, which in Winterspring was a little like adding more ice to a glacier, but it worked. Thun lumbered up the slope and set down an Earthbind Totem and a Stoneskin Totem, one on either side of the fight, with tremendous satisfaction.

Nobody saw Nick arrive. Nick was simply behind the yeti all at once, and then the yeti was stunned and very confused about it.

Wulf hadn't moved from his rock. He drew his bow, loosed one arrow, then a second, and Fram went in low for the hamstring.

And through all of it, Tung stood in front of the fire with his shield up, and didn't move, and let everything that came down that slope come down on the round dull boss at the center of his shield. And it held, because it was always going to hold, because it had been made to.

The yeti went down.

"Nobody," said Tung, lowering his shield, "pull anything else."

"I pulled something else," said Thor.

"*Thor.*"

"It's a *small* something else."

---

The second something else was, in fact, small. It was dealt with. Then the whole Stenbane family stood around a Rich Thorium Vein on a ridge in Winterspring as the sun came up, breathing hard and steaming in the cold, and argued about who got to mine it.

Tung handed Thor the pick.

"You found it," he said. "Twice."

Thor looked at the pick, then at Tung, and then he grinned and swung it.

---

They walked back toward Everlook in a long, ragged line: two dwarves, two gnomes, two humans, a night elf, two Tauren and a wolf. Kobalt was explaining the Voidwalker's contract terms to a deeply uninterested Thun. Tong was showing Penny his bruises. Nick was carrying the camp pot, which Wulf allowed. Thor had the Thorium.

Ting fell into step beside Tung, holding her cold cup in both hands.

"I figured out the second E," she said.

Tung looked down at her.

"For the acronym," she said. "I was stuck on it for years. I figured it out this morning."

"What's it stand for?"

Ting thought about it, and then, deliberately, did not tell him the first seven letters. She just tipped her head at the line of people strung out ahead of them in the snow: tall and small and loud and quiet, Horde and Alliance, and all of them, somehow, named the same thing.

"Everyone," she said.

Tung frowned. "That's not how acronyms work."

"It's how this one works."

---

Tung walked the rest of the way in silence. He thought about a mountain that blinked, and a candle, and a stone in a graveyard, and a form with two boxes on it, and a kodo, and a dream, and a stone with two names that would not melt.

He had come up the mountain this morning wanting an answer. He was going back down without one. And somewhere between the coffee and the yeti, he had found that he didn't mind.

The name didn't need explaining. It needed holding.

That, he could do.

---

At the door of the inn, Penny caught up to him and fell into step on the other side. She had the little leather ledger under her arm.

"Tung," she said sweetly. "I still have the form."

Tung pushed open the door into the warmth and the smell of coffee and Vizzie, already reaching for her pad.

"Put it on the tab," he said.

## Author's Note

The Stenbanes started as a joke about a character creator with two name boxes, and turned into the most fun I've had making alts since 2004.

If you've read this far, you may have spotted the things I couldn't resist. Wulf's uncle, **Krajed**, is my original Tauren Shaman, my main from the Evansville days back in 2004, written in here as the old shaman who handed the tungsten off to the dwarf. Thun is my first Tauren Shaman since him. And *Tungsten* and *Wolfram* really are two names for the same metal: element 74, symbol W, from *wolfram*, with the highest melting point of any metal. One metal, two names. That's the whole family, really.

Happy Thanksgiving from the Stenbanes. If you see one of us in Winterspring, the node is probably already taken.
