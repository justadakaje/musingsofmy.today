## Part 4: The Earthmother and the Dream

Thun went first, because Thor had finally agreed, very graciously, that Tauren had been in Mulgore since before the night elves had noticed Mulgore was there, and Thun had very graciously agreed not to point out how long that had taken.

The Tauren reached into his pack and set a small stone totem in the middle of the table. It was carved with a squat, solid figure, and it sat on the wood like it had grown roots.

"No totems on the table, sugar," said Vizzie.

"It is a very small totem."

"It's a *fire hazard*."

"It is Stoneskin," said Thun. "It is the opposite of a fire hazard."

Vizzie looked at it, then at Thun, who was the size of a door and had braids as thick as her arm, and decided the totem could stay.

---

"When I was a calf," Thun began, "in Mulgore, where the grass is tall and the wind never stops, I climbed to Red Rocks, where my people's ancestors rest. I was to earn my first totem of Earth. I had to listen."

He spoke slowly. He always spoke slowly. Thun treated words like stones in a river crossing, and he tested each one before he put his weight on it.

"I lay down on the red stone, and I pressed my ear to the earth. And I listened for a very long time." He closed his eyes. "And then the Earthmother spoke to me."

The booth went quiet. Even Tong put down his ale.

"She said," said Thun, in a voice like distant thunder, "*Sssssteeeeennnnn*."

Penny drew in a breath.

"And then she said: *Baaaaaaane*."

"That was a kodo," said Tong.

Thun opened one eye. "A kodo does not speak."

"That's what a kodo *wants* you to think."

"The earth spoke," said Thun firmly. "It said *stone*. And then it said: *the one who stands against what would break it*. Bane. Not a breaker of stone. A guard for it."

"That's lovely," said Penny, and meant it.

"Then it said it again," said Thun. "And then it said *mrrrrrrrph*."

There was a long pause.

"That part," Thun admitted, "may have been the kodo."

"*Thank* you," said Tong.

"There may also," said Thun, "have been a kodo for some of the other parts."

---

Thun sat back down, very carefully, and the bench made a noise of relief.

"I hear the earth before I hear people," he said. "That is the shaman's way. The earth told me there would be a family. It did not tell me the family would be this *loud*."

"That's fair," said Ting.

Then Thun said something else, more quietly, almost to himself, the way people do when a memory comes up on its own.

"There was an old shaman of my people. Long before me. He walked the cracks in the world, where the land splits, and listened to them. My grandmother said he once told her the earth would someday need an anvil, not a totem." He shook his great head. "I never understood it. I was very young."

Nobody else at the table seemed to notice.

But across the booth, Wulf's ear turned toward Thun, and stayed there. And at the head of the table, without thinking about it, Tung rested his hand on the cracked leather compass at his belt.

---

"My turn," said Thor.

He didn't stand. Night elves rarely need to stand to be the tallest person in a room. He leaned back against the wall and steepled his long fingers, and the teal of his hair caught the lantern light.

"Druids," he said, "dream."

"Here we go," said Tong.

"Not like you dream. You dream about ale. When a druid sleeps, he walks the Emerald Dream: the world as it was meant to be, before anybody put a mine in it. Every tree is the first tree. Every river runs clean. Every Thorium vein is—"

"Wait," said Kobalt. "There's *Thorium* in the Emerald Dream?"

"There's *everything* in the Emerald Dream."

"Is it minable?"

"That," said Thor, "is exactly the right question, and you will understand my story better than anyone."

"One night," Thor said, "I dreamed of Winterspring. Not this one. The Dream's Winterspring. The snow glowed green and gold. The furbolgs were polite. The Frostsabers let you pet them. And on the side of a ridge, I found a vein of Thorium so rich it was *humming*."

"Ohhh," said Tung, Tong and Kobalt together, reverently.

"The ore ran through the rock in long silver lines," said Thor, "and the lines made *letters*. S. T. E. N. B. A. N. E. Written in Thorium, across the face of the mountain, as if the earth had signed its name."

"I knew it," said Ting under her breath. "It *is* an acronym."

"It's not an acronym. It's a vein."

"It can be both."

"I reached out to touch it," said Thor, "and I woke up."

He paused.

"I was in a snowbank. In the real Winterspring. Right in front of me was a real Rich Thorium Vein, exactly where it had been in the Dream. And standing in front of it, with a pick, mining it—"

Every head turned to the end of the table.

Tung looked into his coffee.

"*Him*," said Thor.

---

"I didn't know you were sleeping there," said Tung.

"I was in the *Dream*."

"You were in a snowbank."

"The Dream *manifests* in snowbanks sometimes."

"You were snoring."

"Druids don't snore. We *commune*."

"You communed loud enough to pull a yeti."

Thor waved a hand. "The point is, I woke up, and a dwarf was mining *my* Thorium vein, the vein I had been *shown*, and I sat up out of the snow and said—"

"*That's my node*," said Tung, flatly.

"And you said?"

Tung sighed.

"*It's Stenbane's node*."

"And I said, *What's a Stenbane?*" Thor held up a long finger. "And you said—"

Tung didn't answer. He turned his mug a quarter turn on the table.

"He said," said Thor, "*Me. You can be one too, if you help carry the ore.*"

"And?" said Penny.

"And I carried the ore," said Thor. "Forty-three trips. Thorium is very heavy. I learned Travel Form *specifically* for the second load." He spread his hands. "So you see, the Dream showed me the name, and the name was standing in front of me with a pick. I don't know where it comes from. But I know where I got it."

---

"So it's *Tung's* name," said Tong.

"The *Dream* had it first," said Thor.

"Night elves," said Thun to no one in particular.

"We *were* here first."

"You say this about *everything*."

"I am going to Moonglade," Thor announced, standing up, "*briefly*," and before anyone could argue he cast Teleport: Moonglade and vanished in a swirl of green light. Eleven minutes later he walked back in through the front door, shaking off snow, carrying a small sack of Thorium ore he had very clearly not gotten in Moonglade, and sat down as though he had never left.

"Druids," said Kobalt.

"Second hearthstone," said Thor serenely.

---

That was the last of the stories for the night. Eight tellings, and not one of them matched another. The fire burned low. Tong fell asleep upright in the booth, still holding his ale, as only a dwarf warrior can. Kobalt's imp curled up in an empty bread basket and smoldered gently. Ting dozed off against Penny's shoulder, and Penny let her.

Vizzie came by one last time and set the tab down in front of Tung. It ran to four pages. In the margins she had drawn a mountain, a candle, a gravestone, a little box with a line through it, a kodo and a vein of ore. At the bottom she'd written a number that Penny looked at, and then folded the paper over so that nobody else had to.

"Name for the tab?" said Vizzie, deadpan.

"Stenbane," said Tung.

"Still don't know what it means, huh?"

"No."

"Huh," said Vizzie, and she smiled, a little, which for a goblin who bills by the hour was practically a hug. "Most families don't, sugar."

---

It was only after she had gone, and the inn had settled into the low creaks and pops of a building full of sleeping people, that Tung looked down the length of the booth and counted heads.

Dwarf. Gnome. Gnome. Human. Human. Night elf. Tauren.

One Tauren.

Thun was asleep with his chin on his chest, snoring like a distant avalanche. The bench beside him, where Wulf had sat all night without saying a single word, was empty. Fram's warm hollow in front of the fire was empty too.

Eight stories tonight, and Wulf hadn't told one.

Outside, the snow was still falling over Winterspring. Through the soles of his boots, faint, Tung felt something he'd learned to feel through plate on stone before every bad pull of his life: a tremor, very far off, like the mountain was thinking about moving.

He finished his coffee. He picked up his shield.
