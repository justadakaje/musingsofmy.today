*The Stenbane Question is a five-part story about a family of WoW: Forever alts. Previously, in [Part 1](/2026/11/12/the-stenbane-question-part-1/): nine Stenbanes crammed into one booth at the Everlook inn, Innkeeper Vizzie asked where the family name came from, and the twins gave two versions of the same story about a mountain. Now the gnomes get their turn.*

---

## Part 2: You No Take Name

Warlocks tell stories the way they cast spells: a long wind-up, a lot of shadow, and at some point something catches fire.

Kobalt stood on the bench with his blue beard jutting out over the table like the prow of a very small ship. The imp had dimmed every lantern within reach, and had lit itself up instead, glowing an unpleasant shade of green, so the gnome's face hovered in the gloom like a ghost story about a dentist.

"Long ago," said Kobalt, "before the Stenbanes were Stenbanes, we had another name. The *old* name. Our *true* name. We were the Fizzlecog-Sprocketwhistle-Gearspinner-Thistlebottom-Copperquill-Von-Tinkerhaus-Gizmoblast."

He took a breath.

"The Third."

"That's ridiculous," said Tong.

"That's the short form. The full name took forty seconds to say and had a gear in the middle of it."

"You can't have a gear in a name," said Ting.

"*Our ancestors could*."

Vizzie, who was still standing at the end of the booth with her pad, wrote down *gear?* and underlined it.

"Our ancestor," Kobalt went on, "was a gnome tunneler of great renown. A surveyor. A visionary. One day he traveled to Elwynn Forest to inspect a mine called Fargodeep."

Everyone at the table who had ever been level eight groaned.

"Yes," said Kobalt. "*Kobolds*."

---

"It was dark in Fargodeep," said Kobalt, lowering his voice. "It is always dark in Fargodeep. Our ancestor had forgotten his lantern, because he had been inventing a better lantern, and it had exploded. So there he was, deep in the mine, in the pitch black, when he saw a light."

The imp, on cue, held up one claw and made a tiny flame on the tip of it.

"A candle. On top of a sleeping kobold's head."

"Oh no," said Penny.

"It was a *very nice* candle," said Kobalt. "Beeswax. Even burn. Our ancestor reasoned that the kobold was asleep and therefore did not need light, and that he was awake and therefore did. Purely logical. So he reached out, very carefully—"

"You no take candle," said Nick.

"—and he lifted the candle off its head, and the kobold opened its eyes—"

"*You no take candle*," said Tong, Thor and Ting together.

"—and it screamed—"

"**YOU NO TAKE CANDLE!**" said the entire booth, including Thun, who arrived about a second after everyone else at full volume and then looked pleased with himself.

A goblin at the bar dropped a glass. Fram lifted his head, decided it wasn't his business, and put it down again.

"Correct," said Kobalt with dignity. "And that would have been the end of it. Except that this kobold was not a common kobold. It was a *Geomancer*."

He let that sit.

"It raised its little claws. The tunnel walls began to glow. And it spoke the curse, in the old kobold tongue, and the curse went like this: *You take candle. We take NAME.*"

"Ominous," said Thor.

"And it took it. All forty seconds of it. The Fizzlecogs, the Sprocketwhistles, the Gearspinners, the Von Tinkerhaus. Gone. Even the gear. Our ancestor opened his mouth to introduce himself to the kobold, as was polite, and found he had nothing to say."

The imp's flame guttered dramatically. Kobalt had clearly rehearsed this with it.

"Then what?" asked Penny, who despite herself had her hands clasped under her chin.

"Then," said Kobalt, "the kobold felt bad."

"It felt *bad*?"

"Kobolds aren't monsters. They're just very particular about candles. And this one looked at a gnome standing in the dark with no name, holding a candle he didn't know what to do with, and it took pity. It said a word in kobold. A new name. To replace the one it had eaten."

He spread his arms.

"*Stenbane*."

---

"It means stone-bane," said Tung.

"In *Dwarvish*," said Kobalt. "In kobold, it means *one who takes the candle and regrets it*."

"Kobolds don't have a language," said Tong. "They have about nine words, and seven of them are 'candle.'"

"Kobolds have *opinions*. Same thing."

"So we're named after a candle," said Tung.

"We are named after the *consequences* of a candle," said Kobalt. "Very different. And ask any old miner. There is an ore, blue, ill-tempered, poisons the smelter, ruins your bellows, and the miners of old named it after the kobolds they blamed for cursing it." He tapped his own chest. "*Kobalt*. I am literally named after the family curse. I didn't become a warlock by accident. I became a warlock by inheritance."

"Is that why you summon things?" asked Penny.

"I summon things because I have *contracts*," said Kobalt. "Several. All very binding. I have a Soul Shard bag that would make you weep."

"Please don't show us the Soul Shard bag," said Thor.

"It's *organized*."

"We know. You showed us last Winter Veil. For an hour."

The imp snickered and muttered something in Demonic that made the lantern beside Thor go briefly purple. Kobalt flicked its ear without looking.

Nick reached into his coat and set something on the table.

It was a candle.

Everyone stared at it.

"What?" said Nick. "It was on the table."

It was the inn's candle. Penny picked it up and put it back where it belonged, and Vizzie added a line to the tab anyway on principle.

"Candle," Vizzie said, writing. "Curse. Kobold. Got it. Next."

---

"That's wrong," said a small voice. "You're all wrong."

Ting had her hands folded on the table in front of her. She had been quiet all through the mountain and the candle, sipping her own conjured water, and now she looked around the booth with the patient contempt of the youngest person in a family who is also, privately, the smartest.

"It's an acronym."

There was a pause.

"What?" said Tong.

"*Stenbane*. It's an acronym. S-T-E-N-B-A-N-E. Every letter stands for something."

"For what?"

"I'm not telling you."

The pause got longer.

"You can't do that," said Kobalt. "You can't just *have* an acronym and not say what it stands for."

"I can, actually. That's what *classified* means."

---

What followed lasted about ten minutes and is best summarized.

"Strong, Tough, Extremely Nice Beards And—" said Tong.

"No."

"Shadow Tinkers Eternally Negotiating Binding Arcane—" said Kobalt.

"*No*."

"Seriously Tall Elves Need Bigger—" said Thor.

"Absolutely not. And you only got to B."

Thun raised a hand, slowly. "Spirits... Tell... Everyone... Names... Before..."

"Thun, that's lovely, and no."

"Is it something nice?" Penny asked.

Ting thought about that for longer than anyone expected.

"It's something *accurate*," she said.

"Somebody give me a letter," said Nick. "One letter. I'll figure out the rest."

"You'll steal the rest, you mean."

"Same thing, at my level."

Tung hadn't joined the guessing. He sat with his coffee and watched Ting, the way he watched a doorway when a pull was about to come through it.

She was the youngest of them, and she had come to the family last, after Gnomeregan fell. Nobody talked much about that. She had been very small and very sooty and very angry, and she'd arrived in Ironforge holding on to something nobody was allowed to see. She still kept it somewhere. Tung had never asked what.

"When you lose a whole city," Ting said, not looking at anyone in particular, "you get particular about what words mean. That's all."

The booth went quiet. Even Tong.

"So who gave it to you?" asked Thor gently. "The acronym."

"It doesn't matter who gave it," said Ting. "What matters is what it stands for."

"Which is?"

Ting took a long sip of water.

"Classified."

---

"Okay," said Vizzie. She had flipped to a fresh page. "So far I got a mountain, the same mountain but headbutted, a candle, a curse, and a secret code." She looked at the length of the booth. "I'm gonna need a bigger tab."

"Put it on the tab," said Tung.

"The tab *is* the tab, sugar."

Wulf said nothing. Fram yawned enormously, showing every tooth he owned, and rolled over to warm the other side.

At the far end of the booth, Nick stretched, cracked his knuckles one at a time, and leaned back against the wall with his hands behind his head.

"Alright," he said. "My turn. You want the truth?"

"Oh, no," said Penny.

"The truth," said Nick, "is that I stole it."
