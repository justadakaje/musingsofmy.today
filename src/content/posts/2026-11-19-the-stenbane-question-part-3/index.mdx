## Part 3: Grave Matters

"You stole it," said Tung.

"Borrowed," said Nick. "Long-term."

He had the floor now, and he took it the way he took everything else: casually, and before anybody noticed. He tipped his chair back on two legs, which was impressive, given that he was sitting on a bench.

"This was years back," he said. "I had a misunderstanding in Stormwind. With a merchant in the Trade District, over a very small amount of money."

"How small?" asked Penny.

"Very small. It was his."

Penny closed her eyes.

"So I took a little trip," Nick went on. "South. Through Elwynn. Across the river. Into Duskwood, where it's always night and every tree looks like it's about to tell you some bad news. Lovely place to lie low. Nobody goes to Duskwood on purpose."

"I've been to Duskwood on purpose," said Thor.

"Did you enjoy it?"

Thor thought about this. "No."

"There you go."

---

"Problem was," said Nick, "Darkshire has the Night Watch, and the Night Watch has a gate, and the gate has a fellow with a lantern and a ledger who wants to know your name before he lets you in. Your *whole* name. And I didn't have a surname I wanted written down in any ledger within a hundred miles of Stormwind."

"So you lied," said Tong.

"So I *improvised*. Up the road there's a place called Raven Hill. There's a cemetery there. Very old, very full, and very poorly supervised. I figured: graveyard, lot of names, most of them not in use."

"Nick," said Penny, slowly, "*please* tell me you didn't—"

"Middle of the night. Fog on the ground. Worgen howling out in the trees. Skeletons doing whatever skeletons do, mostly falling apart and getting back up. And there, at the back of the cemetery, in the moss, I find this little stone. Tilted over. So old the first name had worn clean off. But the last name, you could still read it." He drew it in the air with one finger. "*Here lies* blank *Stenbane*."

"You stole a dead man's name," said Penny.

"He wasn't using it."

"*Nick!*"

"I *checked*," said Nick. "Twice. Second time a skeleton climbed out of the next grave over, and I asked it straight: *You using this?* And it said nothing. Which, in Duskwood, I'm fairly sure counts as permission."

"That's not how permission works," said Penny.

"That's how permission works in *Duskwood*."

---

Kobalt had gone very still, the way warlocks do when they hear the word *grave* in a sentence they like.

"Which cemetery plot?" he said. "Row? Approximate location? Purely for... research."

"No," said Penny, Tung and Thun at once.

"I'd *put it back*."

"*No*."

"The dead remember," Thun said solemnly, and for a moment the booth felt a few degrees colder, which might also have been the door opening.

"Hold on," said Thor. "If there was already a Stenbane buried in Duskwood, then who was *that*?"

Everybody looked at Nick. Nick shrugged.

"Never found out. The gate guard wrote down *Nick Stenbane*, and I spent a very peaceful season in Darkshire not being arrested. Then I wandered north, and about a year later, I walked into Ironforge, and a dwarf with a shield the size of a door looked down at me and said—"

He nodded down the table.

"*That's my name*," said Tung.

"And instead of breaking me in half," said Nick, "he bought me a drink. That's how I knew it was family. Family's whoever shares your name and doesn't stab you."

"That," said Tung, "is not how I remember it."

"How do you remember it?"

"You stole my coin purse. I caught you. I bought you a drink because you'd already paid for it with my money."

Nick considered this.

"Same story," he said. "Different angle."

---

Vizzie wrote *grave robbing (borrowing?)* on the tab, and then, after a moment, a small surcharge she seemed to have added out of habit.

"Okay," she said. "That's five. Who's—"

"Me," said Penny.

She hadn't raised her voice. She had simply opened the little leather ledger, the family purse ledger, the one with every bill, every repair, every Winter Veil apology letter carefully written down in her small, neat hand. Tucked inside the back cover was a folded sheet of thicker paper. She drew it out, unfolded it, and laid it flat in the middle of the table, smoothing the creases with both palms.

"I didn't want to do this," she said. "But I'm tired of the canal."

Everybody leaned in.

It was a page from the clan registry in Ironforge, the copy they keep in the Hall of Explorers for scholars and genealogists and anybody willing to pay a clerk two silver to stop pretending he hadn't heard you.

It was a form. A plain, ink-stamped Ironforge form, with two boxes printed side by side across the top.

The first box said **GIVEN NAME.**

The second box said **CLAN NAME.**

And across both of them, in thick, heavy, unmistakably dwarven capitals, pressed so hard the nib had gone through the paper in two places, somebody had written a single word straight over the line between the boxes as if the line were merely a suggestion:

**TUNG | STENBANE**

Underneath, in a tidy gnomish hand, the registry clerk had noted: *Entered as written. New clan. Registered.* And beneath that, the stamp: a red-wax anvil, slightly smudged.

The booth was very quiet.

"It's not a curse," said Penny gently. "It's not a mountain, and it's not a candle, and it's not a grave. It's paperwork. Tung wrote his name too big, the clerk wrote down what was in each box, and that was that. Every Stenbane since then just..." She made a small, helpless gesture down the table. "Joined."

---

The silence held for one more second.

Then the whole booth burst out laughing.

"*Paperwork?*" said Tong, wiping his eyes. "That's the dumbest one yet!"

"Where's the *drama*?" said Kobalt. "Where's the *curse*? Where's the *candle*?"

"In what world," said Thor, "is a family named by *clerical error*?"

"The Earthmother," said Thun, with great dignity, "does not use forms."

Tung had turned an interesting color under his beard.

"I did not," he said stiffly, "write my name across two boxes."

"You absolutely did," said Tong. "You always do. You wrote across *three* boxes on your Blacksmithing guild application."

"That was a long name."

"It was your *own name*."

Nick had picked up the page and was holding it up to the lantern, tilting it, running a thumb over the wax with the frown of a professional.

"Seal's real," he said at last.

"*Thank* you," said Penny.

"That's what bothers me." Nick set the page down. "If it were forged, I'd believe it."

---

Penny folded the registry page back up, very neatly, and tucked it into the back of the ledger where it lived.

"Fine," she said, and took a sip of her tea. "The only version with a stamp on it. And nobody believes it."

Whether that page was real is a question the family has argued about ever since. It is the only version of the story that comes with a seal and a clerk's signature. It has never helped.

Tung stared into his coffee for a long moment. Then, so quietly that only Penny heard it, he said, "The line between the boxes was very faint."

Penny patted his arm.

---

Later, when she went to settle the first round with Vizzie, Penny opened the family purse and paused. It was heavier than it had been when she'd come in. Considerably heavier. There was a gold ring in it that hadn't been there before, a handful of silver, and a small Stormwind coin she was fairly sure had last been seen in Tong's boot.

She looked down the booth.

Nick was examining the ceiling.

"Where did this come from?" she asked.

"No idea," said Nick.

Wulf said nothing. But one ear turned, very slightly, toward Nick, and then back toward the fire.

---

Thun stood up.

This was an event. A standing Tauren in a goblin inn is a weather system, and the rafters of the Everlook inn had not been built with weather in mind. Thun ducked his horns under the beam, straightened as far as he could, and laid one broad hand flat on the table.

"Now," he said. "The *true* story."

"After mine," said Thor, who was also on his feet, and nearly as tall.

"You may go after."

"I was here first."

Thun looked at him with deep patience. "You are a night elf. You say this about everything."

"Because it's *usually true*."
