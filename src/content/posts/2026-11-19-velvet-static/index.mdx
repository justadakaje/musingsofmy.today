This started as a 2 a.m. prompt experiment: asking Spotify AI for *"the most fuzzy buzzy, neuro bass laced electronic music."* Spiritual cousin to [FebruFuzzz](/2024/02/17/februfuzzz/) and [Mid Summer Static](/2025/08/05/experience-the-sound-of-late-summer-mid-summer-static/), pushed deeper into sub-bass territory.

To Spotify AI's credit, it skipped the obvious trap. Instead of dumping 30 tracks of relentless 174 BPM mainroom neurofunk, it built a surprisingly cohesive 30-track draft that got roughly **85%** of the aesthetic right: psychedelic halftime, organic downtempo experimental bass, and murky UK 140 orbiting the worlds of **KOAN Sound**, **Chmura**, **CloZee**, **Saturna**, **The Widdler**, **Mystic State**, **Mindex**, **Ferrofluid**, and **Swomp**.

Here is the catch with vector-similarity playlists: 85% is right where algorithms stop. Turning a good pile of tracks into a playlist you actually want to press play on from Track 1 to the final reverb tail means auditing what broke the spell and sequencing the energy like a real set. Even better: I happened to be away from my desktop until Friday night, so the entire audit, cover-art build, live Spotify deployment, cross-platform sync, and this blog post became a real-world stress test of **Google Antigravity** running its newly launched **Orchestrator (Neon)** model—driven completely from a PWA on my iPhone.

## The Full Phone-Only Workflow: Spotify AI → Antigravity (Neon) → Multi-Platform

What started as casual late-night listening turned into a clean four-stage pipeline—no laptop screen, no manual track-dragging, and zero plaintext credentials left on disk.

### 1. The Spark & JSON Handoff

First, I prompted Spotify's AI playlist chat for fuzzy, neuro-bass electronic music, let it spin up **Fuzzy Neuro Bass**, and asked it to save the session. Instead of screenshotting thirty tracks one by one, I opened **SongShift** on my phone and exported the saved playlist as a structured `Fuzzy Neuro Bass.json` payload—giving me clean track names, albums, artists, ISRCs, and Spotify IDs in seconds.

### 2. Auditing & Sculpting in Google Antigravity (`Orchestrator (Neon)`)

I pasted that raw JSON straight into **Google Antigravity** running **Orchestrator (Neon)**—Google's dual-phase planning-and-execution workflow powered by the brand-new **Gemini 4 Neon** model. Inside a single continuous thread, Antigravity researched tempos, harmonic keys, subgenres, and label relationships across all 30 tracks, flagged three genre-bleed outliers, researched eight studio-authentic additions to supply the missing neuro synthesis, rebuilt the entire running order into a five-act arc, and handed off cover generation to an image subagent to produce a 1:1 macro ferrofluid-and-moss sculpture with zero text or DJ clichés.

### 3. The Away-From-Desk Constraint (Zero-Secret PKCE Pivot)

Once the curation report looked right, I asked Antigravity to push **Velvet Static** live on Spotify instead of leaving me to drag 35 tracks around manually. That hit an immediate real-world constraint: because I was away from my desk, hardware-backed local password-manager biometric approvals on the home workstation couldn't be clicked remotely.

Rather than waiting until Friday night or falling back to plaintext keys, Antigravity pivoted cleanly to **Spotify's Authorization Code with PKCE flow**:

- **Zero secrets over chat or on disk:** Using only my public app identifier, the local script generated an ephemeral SHA-256 `code_verifier` held strictly in memory, sent me a single-use authorization link, and waited for the phone browser redirect URL.
- **Feb 2026 Spotify Web API compliance:** Exchanging that one-time authorization code strictly in RAM, the script resolved all eight new tracks against Spotify's updated `limit=10` Search behavior (verifying exact artist and original studio release matches before creating anything), created public **Velvet Static** via `/v1/me/playlists`, added all 35 URIs in exact five-act sequence via `/items`, compressed the custom cover artwork down to **98,924** Base64 bytes under Spotify's 256 KB ceiling, uploaded it, read everything back to verify **35/35** tracks and active cover art, and discarded the temporary token on exit.

### 4. Mirroring Back Out & Reviewing From an iPhone PWA

The moment **Velvet Static** went live on Spotify with its custom cover and 35-track sequence locked, two taps inside **SongShift** replicated the exact playlist—custom artwork and running order included—over to **Apple Music** and **YouTube**. Meanwhile, Antigravity scaffolded and validated this post directly inside my Astro repo, letting me inspect inline draft artifacts and `+2 -2` file diffs right inside the Google Antigravity **PWA on my iPhone**—zero laptop required.

## What Spotify AI Nailed—and Where Embeddings Bleed

What Spotify AI understood immediately was *texture*. Warm sine-compressed low-end, micro-edited percussion, mossy soundscapes, and deep sub pressure across Qualia, Odyzey, Gravitas, Shoshin, and Chikara Project orbits showed up naturally.

Where the raw 30-track draft stumbled boiled down to three classic algorithmic failure modes (if you want to A/B test it yourself, the [original Fuzzy Neuro Bass playlist](https://open.spotify.com/playlist/4Bz9u2rOV0gCAldyHM8NX7) is still untouched):

1. **Embedding bleed (the three cuts):** Prompting with *"fuzzy"* and *"dark"* dragged in three adjacent-genre outliers that broke the rhythmic pocket:
   - **Jahrund – *Blue Light Hunter*** (originally #1): German dark-pop and synthwave with alt-pop vocal framing. Opening a deep bass mix here immediately sets the wrong room.
   - **SIERRA VEINS – *Gone*** (originally #9): French darksynth / EBM wedged directly between two 140 bass tracks (**Dank Frank** and **Jungle Rootz**). That rigid industrial drum grid collides hard with swinging halftime subs.
   - **Shadient – *NOT ANYMORE*** (originally #28): Harsh, brickwalled industrial abrasion dropped right in front of meditative UK 140 (**Mystic State**).

   Funny enough, **two of those cuts were tracks I genuinely enjoyed on the initial playthrough**. That is the quiet paradox of curating a sequence: a song can be great on its own and still sabotage the two tracks beside it. Keeping the original Spotify AI draft intact preserves those outliers where they belong, while **Velvet Static** commits to the cohesive lane.

2. **Whiplash pacing:** **Robot Koch's** ambient *Instructions for Time Travel (Recue Remix)* got burned at #6 like a random speed bump; **Kindrid & Syberlilly's** misty *Autumn Fog* landed stranded right after **The Widdler's** 140 dubstep weight; and the whole draft ended abruptly on peak-energy **Mersiv – *Digital Eden*** with no exhale.

3. **Unspaced artist and label overlaps:** **Daggz** appeared twice in four slots (*Transmutation* with **Chmura** at #21, then *Magicians* at #25), **Lowcation** appeared twice (*Air Bender* and the *Recurring Dreams* remix), and **Huck.Jorris** showed up in both *Empathica* and **Saturna's** *Promise* remix. Meanwhile, Chikara Project labelmates **Jungle Rootz** (*Catharsis*) and **Mystic State** (*The Last Lotus*) belong back-to-back as a deep 140 pivot rather than scattered nineteen tracks apart.

## Earning the "Neuro" Half: The Eight Additions

The biggest surprise in the audit was how lightly Spotify AI actually touched *neuro* sound design. Roughly 75% of the original draft leaned psychedelic/downtempo space bass, while only a handful of tracks—**KOAN Sound**, **Monuman**, **IMANU**, **Mindex**, and **Cut Rugs**—carried true neuro-style synthesis and modulation.

To earn the name without abandoning the warm, fuzzy hypnosis of the original core, I added **eight tracks** across tempo hinges and peak textures:

| Artist | Track | Approx BPM / Feel | Role in the Sequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CharlestheFirst** | *Mercy Falls* | 80 / 160 BPM | Anchors the emotional organic-halftime spine straight into **Chmura** |
| **Supertask** | *Zero Day* | 110 BPM | Tactile, fuzzy midtempo bridge stepping cleanly out of **CloZee – *LOST*** |
| **Tipper** | *Cubic Squeal* | 88 / 176 BPM | Peak squelchy neuro sound design locked alongside **Mindex – *Zen*** |
| **Vorso** | *The Imperative* | ~86 / 172 BPM | Surgical halftime neuro-bass matching **KOAN Sound's** precision |
| **Of The Trees** | *Spanish Moss* | 154 / 77 HT | Missing organic centerpiece linking **Monuman** into his **Pigeon Hole** collab |
| **Chee** | *Vultures* | 140 BPM | Writhing, razor-edged neuro textures locked natively to 140 |

## Telemetry Update (v2): Re-Sequencing From Spotify's Real Beat Grid

Live-testing **v1** on the flight back home from **SFO** with Spotify's **Mixed** feature enabled confirmed the vibe worked—and once I got back to my desk and opened Spotify Desktop's full Mixed view, seeing the actual beat-grid BPMs, Camelot keys, and spectral waveforms exposed six places where public web BPM databases had lied to us:

| Track | Artist | Public Web Databases | Spotify Mixed Grid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Instructions for Time Travel - Recue Remix* | Robot Koch, Savannah Jo Lack, Recue | 99 BPM | **168 BPM** (84 HT · `7A`) |
| *Zero Day* | Supertask | 110 BPM | **128 BPM** (`9A`) |
| *Withholding* | Ferrofluid | 116 BPM | **134 BPM** (`3B`) |
| *Alchemy* | Swomp | ~120–124 BPM | **140 BPM** (`4A`) |
| *Cubic Squeal* | Tipper | 88 / 176 BPM | **143 BPM** (`5B`) |
| *Type 33* | Dank Frank | 140 BPM | **80 BPM** (160 DT · `9A`) |

With **zero cuts and zero new tracks**, moving just five seams unlocked **14 clean Camelot harmonic transitions** across the set:

- **Act II smoothing & the `3B` twin:** Pulling **Swomp** (`140 · 4A`) out of Act II smoothed the midtempo ramp into `2A → 1A → 1A`, paired **Ferrofluid** (`134 · 3B`) directly into **Kindrid** (`135 · 3B`), and swapped **IMANU → Cut Rugs** (`120 · 8A`) to hand off `8A → 9A` cleanly into **Mindex**.
- **Halftime Act III lock:** Moving **Tipper** (`143 · 5B`) up to Act IV and pulling **Dank Frank** (`80 · 9A`) into Act III turned the entire neuro-halftime core into `Mindex (90 · 9A) → KOAN Sound (172/86 · 8A) → Vorso (170/85 · 9A) → Dank Frank (80 · 9A) → Monuman (155/77.5 · 3A) → Of The Trees (77 · 12B)`—every transition within ≤5 BPM.
- **Two Act IV harmonic pockets:** **Pigeon Hole → Swomp → Ashez → Mersiv** locked an opening `4A → 4A → 3A → 4A` cluster, while grouping **Lowcation** (`142 · 7B`) right ahead of **Chee** (`140 · 7A`) built a five-track **`7B → 7A → 7A → 8A → 7B`** gauntlet through **The Widdler**, **Alix Perez**, and **Jungle Rootz**.
- **Pure deceleration finale:** Putting **Saturna** (`124 · 11A`) at #35 closed the playlist cleanly on a downward slope (`140 → 132 → 132 → 124`).
