*The Stenbane Question is a five-part story about a family of World of Warcraft alts, a goblin innkeeper, and a surname nobody can explain. New parts every Monday and Thursday, finishing on Thanksgiving.*

## Behind the Scenes: Twelve Letters, Two Boxes

Every WoW character I've cared about has had a name that meant something.

In 2004, in my Evansville days, it was **Krajed** (say it "caged"), a Tauren Shaman wandering Mulgore when the whole world was still unmapped. I played him all the way through *Wrath of the Lich King* before I moved on to a different character and left him behind. Then career, marriage and family took over, and I stepped away for a long stretch, roughly the original *Mists of Pandaria* through *Legion*. When I came back, it was for *Classic Wrath*, playing alongside my daughter, Ava, and I did something I had never done: I rolled Alliance, and I rolled a Paladin.

That was **Tungstenbane**. Dwarf, Protection, Mankrik. He carried me through *Classic Wrath* and into *Cataclysm*, and somewhere along the way he found a home in the *Sassy Fiends* guild. He was named after tungsten, the metal with the highest melting point there is, because his whole job was to not melt. Later, when we jumped into the *WoW: Forever* beta together, I rolled a Skyborn Elf Hunter, **Aeldorath Zephrai**. When *Forever* went live for real, he rerolled as **Aeldorath Cirrostratus**.

Then *WoW: Forever* arrived, and its character creator asks for a **first name** and a **last name**. Twelve characters maximum in each box. *Tungstenbane* is exactly twelve letters. It would have fit. But two boxes are two boxes, and the guild had already made the cut for me: in *Sassy Fiends*, nobody ever called me Tungstenbane. I was always just "Tung." So the split was right there:

**Tung Stenbane.**

Once he had a surname, he needed a family.

### The naming game

I brainstormed the alts with Antigravity, the AI agent I use for addon work, under one rule: every first name had to be a metal, an ore, or a forge word, cut short the way *Tung* is cut from *Tungsten*. Not every idea survived. *Wolfram Stenbane* does not roll off the tongue, and at one point the Tauren's first-name box read *Malort*, which is also the name of a famously bitter Swedish liquor. Here's who made it:

| Name | Race / Class | Where the name came from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tung** | Dwarf Paladin | Tungsten. The patriarch. Miner and Blacksmith. |
| **Tong** | Dwarf Warrior | Tongs, the smith's tool. Tung's twin. |
| **Ting** | Gnome Mage | Tung, Tong, Ting: the smallest one gets the smallest vowel. |
| **Kobalt** | Gnome Warlock | Cobalt is named after *kobolds*, the mine goblins that German miners blamed for bad ore. Blue hair, naturally. |
| **Nick** | Human Rogue | Nickel. Also what he does to your coin purse. |
| **Penny** | Human Priest | Copper. Nick pockets the coins; Penny gives them away. Copper hair, naturally. |
| **Thor** | Night Elf Druid | Thorium, because I love mining it in Winterspring. |
| **Thun** | Tauren Shaman | **Thun**der + **Tung**. My first Tauren Shaman since Krajed. |
| **Wulf** | Tauren Hunter | Wolfram, tungsten's other name, cut down to something sayable. |

Nine names, nine classes, five races, both factions. A family that by all rights shouldn't be one, which raises an obvious question. This story is my attempt to avoid answering it.

## Part 1: Name for the Tab?

Everlook was the only town in Winterspring where a Dwarf Paladin and a Tauren Shaman could share a table without anybody calling the guards, mostly because the guards were goblins and the goblins were paid by the hour.

So that's where the Stenbanes held the reunion.

Tung arrived first. He always arrived first, because arriving first meant choosing the table, and choosing the table meant choosing which wall to put his back against. He took the corner booth of the inn, leaned his shield against the bench where his hand would find it without looking, and asked Innkeeper Vizzie for the darkest coffee she had.

It came out bitter enough to strip rust off a mailbox. He nodded at it, approving.

He was a broad dwarf even by dwarf standards, dark-skinned, with a black beard that came to a point at his belt and a mustache he'd braided and curled up at the ends like a pair of tongs. On his belt, next to his mining pick, hung an old leather-bound compass with a cracked face. Nobody in the family had ever seen him read it. Nobody had ever asked. Tung had a talent for making questions feel like they'd cost more than they were worth.

He was here for the Thorium. That was the official reason. Thor had sent word from Winterspring: rich veins along the ridges, the Frostsabers thinning out, the Winterfall furbolgs in a mood but manageable.

The unofficial reason was that it had been far too long since the whole family had been under one roof, and Tung was not the kind of dwarf who said things like that out loud. He said "Thorium" instead. Everybody understood.

---

The door banged open at a quarter past ten, and a second Tung walked in.

Same face. Same beard. Same mustache, braided and curled. The only differences were the trousers (bright yellow, with suspenders) and the volume.

"I'm older," Tong announced to the room.

"By four minutes," said Tung, without looking up.

"Four minutes of *experience*." Tong dropped into the booth hard enough to rattle the coffee. "Ale!"

"It's ten in the morning."

"It's evening somewhere. Probably Stranglethorn."

Vizzie brought the ale. She had run an inn in Winterspring long enough to know that you don't argue with a dwarf about what time it is.

---

Penny came next. She stepped in out of the snow in white and pale-blue robes, shaking flakes out of copper-red hair, and before the door had swung shut behind her she had cast Power Word: Fortitude on Tung, Tong, Innkeeper Vizzie, a goblin bruiser by the door, and a cat asleep near the fire.

The bruiser looked at his arms, which were suddenly much harder to hurt, and then at Penny.

"You looked tired," she said.

Penny carried the family purse. Nobody had voted on this. It had simply become true the way most things in a family become true, one awkward bill at a time. She set a small leather ledger on the table, squared it to the edge, and sat down.

"Where's Nick?" she asked.

"Here," said Nick, who was already sitting in the booth.

Nobody had seen him come in. Nobody ever did. He was a lean, older human, dark-skinned, grey at the temples and grey in his stubble, with the posture of a man who knew exactly where every exit was and had already used two of them that morning.

Tung looked down. His coffee was gone.

Penny held out her hand without a word. Nick sighed and placed the mug in it. She gave it back to Tung.

"I was going to give it back," Nick said.

"You always say that."

"And I always do. Eventually."

---

The gnomes arrived in a small cloud of smoke, which turned out to be the menu.

"That was the imp," said Kobalt, waving at the flames. "Bad habit. We're working on it." He was an older gnome in a crimson robe, with a shock of blue hair standing straight up and a blue beard waxed to a sharp point, and the imp scampering at his heels looked smug about the menu in a way that suggested they weren't working on it at all.

Vizzie wrote something on her pad.

"Put it on the tab," said Kobalt.

"Already did, sugar."

Behind him came Ting, small even for a gnome, in a purple robe, with short black hair, gold earrings and the expression of someone who very much wanted to be taken seriously. She climbed onto the bench, folded her hands, and opened her mouth to say something serious.

"Ting," said Tong, "can I get some water?"

"Me too," said Penny.

"And me," said Nick.

"I'm a mage," said Ting, "not a well."

She conjured six waters anyway. She always did.

---

Thor came in from the cold carrying half of Winterspring on his shoulders and the other half on his boots. He was tall even for a night elf, violet-skinned, with long teal hair and a band of dark markings across his eyes, and his hands were grey with ore dust.

"Found three Rich Thorium veins on the way in," he said.

"Mine them?" asked Tung.

"Two." Thor sat down, folding his long legs under the table like a heron settling in. "Third one's yours. Behind the yeti caves. Don't tell anyone."

Tung, very slightly, smiled into his coffee.

---

The last two ducked under the doorframe together, and the whole inn went quiet for a moment, the way rooms do when a quarter-ton of Tauren arrives twice.

Thun and Wulf looked almost exactly alike: black fur, long braids bound in leather, a gold ring through the nose, horns curving out wide enough to clear a table. People who met them both usually spent the first hour unsure which was which.

The way to tell them apart was simple. Thun talked. Wulf did not.

"Greetings, family," said Thun. He spoke Common the way a kodo walks on ice: slowly, and with total commitment.

Wulf nodded. That was Wulf's whole greeting, and from Wulf it was considered warm. His wolf, Fram, padded in after him, turned three circles in front of the fire, and lay down on Kobalt's imp.

"What if the wolf is dismissed?" Ting had asked once, years ago, about telling them apart.

"Then you wait," Tung had said.

---

So there they were, all nine of them, crammed into a corner booth built for four: two dwarves, two gnomes, two humans, a night elf and two Tauren. Alliance on one side, Horde on the other, a goblin inn in the middle, and a single bill.

Innkeeper Vizzie came over with her pad.

"Okay," she said. "Name for the tab?"

"Stenbane," said Tung.

"Which one?"

Nine voices: "All of us."

Vizzie looked down the booth. Dwarf, dwarf, gnome, gnome, human, human, night elf, Tauren, Tauren. One wolf.

"Huh," she said. "Stenbane. What's that even mean? *Stone*-bane? You folks break rocks for a living or something?"

"Some of us," said Tung.

Vizzie held her pen above the pad. "So where's it come from?"

---

Nobody spoke.

It's worth explaining that nobody in the family ever asked this question. It wasn't a secret. It was worse than a secret. Everybody had an answer, and no two answers matched. The last time it came up, over Winter Veil dinner at a tavern in the Ironforge Commons, the evening ended with Tong in the canal, Kobalt's imp banned for life, and Penny writing a letter of apology to a city.

"Take your time," said Vizzie. "I bill by the hour."

Tung set down his mug.

"I'll go first," he said. "Since it's my name."

"*Our* name," said Tong.

"I had it first."

"By four minutes."

Tung ignored him with the ease of long practice.

"In the old tongue," he said, "*sten* means stone. A long time ago, before Ironforge had gates, one of our ancestors walked up into the mountains of Dun Morogh and found a mountain in his way."

"Great-grandsire Brokk," Tong supplied.

"A mountain," Tung went on. "Not a hill. Most dwarves would have dug through it. But Brokk didn't have his pick that day."

"Why not?" asked Thor.

"He'd lent it to a gnome."

"Rude," said Ting.

"So he sat down in front of the mountain, and he told it to move. It didn't. So he waited."

"How long?" asked Penny.

"Eleven years."

Kobalt frowned. "What did he eat?"

"Stubbornness."

"Very filling," Tong agreed.

"On the first morning of the twelfth year," said Tung, "the mountain moved four feet to the left. You can still see the gap. Later on, dwarves built a gate in it. Some people call it the entrance to Ironforge."

He picked up his coffee.

"So they called him Stenbane. The bane of stone. Not because he broke it. Because he outlasted it."

There was a respectful silence. The fire popped. Fram sighed in his sleep, and somewhere underneath him, the imp sighed too.

"That's beautiful," said Penny.

"That's not true," said Nick.

"It's true enough to hold weight," said Tung.

---

"He's telling it wrong," said Tong.

"I'm telling it exactly right."

"You left out the important part." Tong leaned in, and the whole booth leaned with him, because Tong had that effect on rooms. "Brokk didn't *sit*. Brokk *charged* it."

"He charged a mountain."

"Repeatedly."

"Charge has a cooldown," said Ting.

"Not back then it didn't. Back then, Charge had *heart*." Tong thumped his chest. "He ran at that mountain every morning for eleven years. Headfirst. And on the first morning of the twelfth year, the mountain moved four feet to the left. Not because it got tired of waiting." He spread his hands. "Because it got tired of *Brokk*."

"Bane of stone," said Thor slowly. "Because he headbutted it until it gave up."

"*Exactly*."

Tung sipped his coffee. "That explains a great deal about your side of the family."

"We're twins."

"That explains even more."

---

Vizzie had been writing all of this down, and now she looked up from a page covered in arrows.

"So which one do I put on the tab?"

"Put Stenbane," said Tung.

"That's not an answer, sugar. That's the question."

Tung thought about it, and gave the reply a Protection Paladin gives to most of life's problems: he absorbed it and kept standing.

"Then put it on the tab."

---

Across the booth, a large black hand rose slowly into the air.

"I also know where the name comes from," said Thun.

"No, *I'm* next," said Kobalt, already on his feet on the bench, which put him roughly at Thun's elbow. "I have the oldest version. I have *documentation*."

At the end of the table, very quietly, Penny closed her little leather ledger and laid her hand flat on top of it.

"So do I," she said.

Nobody heard her. Nick did, but Nick heard everything, and kept most of it.

Wulf said nothing.

Kobalt cleared his throat. His imp wriggled out from under the wolf and dimmed the nearest lantern with a snap of its fingers, and the shadows in the corner booth stretched out long and blue.

"It began," said Kobalt, "with a candle."
