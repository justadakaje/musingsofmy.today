At **8:21 p.m. ET on Friday, November 13, 2026**, an ADS-B and satellite reconstruction by `@bilawal.ai` (*God's Eye View*, shown above) caught U.S. airspace almost empty: **875 aircraft airborne**, **8 departures in five minutes**, and **+2,883 more aircraft than normal sitting on the ground** at hubs like `DFW +142`, `ATL +124`, `LAX +110`, `IAH +103`, `JFK +101`, `IAD +81`, `MCO +80`, `CLT +71`, and `SFO +50`. The FAA's nationwide ground stop ran from **3:54 to 8:15 p.m. EST** and produced **over 10,000 delays** and **over 4,000 cancellations** that day, and **15,000 to 18,000 disrupted flights** across the weekend.

It was one of six major U.S. outages in six days. Microsoft 365, the FAA, JPMorgan Chase and the Zelle/Plaid rails, United Airlines, Apple, and the NSA's classified network all went down between **November 12 and 17**. Officials ruled out a cyberattack in every case.

Then came the explanation. President Trump: *"Tell them to relax, everything is good, these are individual events,"* and *"We have thousands of companies that are upgrading right now, and when they upgrade, bad things happen."* A week later, Meta Chief AI Officer **Alexandr Wang** published **"Superintelligence is Arriving,"** and social feeds compressed the two into one message: AI is moving so fast that we should get used to things breaking.

I don't buy it, and the post-mortems don't support it. Every cause disclosed so far is a **specific deployment-safety control that failed**: a router setting nobody caught, a test lab smaller than production, a patch that didn't come back cleanly, a bug that spread through shared payment rails. Those are engineering failures with engineering fixes.

*This is Part 1 of 2, published before the **Dec 2, 2026** Senate Commerce hearing "Preventing the Next Ground Stop," where Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford are expected to testify. Part 2 will score their answers against the questions at the end of this post.*

## 2. The Router in El Dorado

The early Day-1 story (*CBS News*, *The Wall Street Journal*) was that a production network update on **L3Harris**'s **FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure (FTI)** caused router reboots and a crash. **L3Harris** has run FTI since 2002 and says its copper-to-fiber conversion is **more than 70%** complete.

<Figure src={l3harrisUpdateImg} alt="Threads post by @professionallyferalgenx detailing the L3Harris production network update, reboot, crash, and four unaddressed segmentation and rollback questions">
  Figure 2: On Day 1, `@professionallyferalgenx` asked exactly the right questions: why did one network update reach every center, where was the segmentation, and was there a tested rollback?
</Figure>

Over the following week the picture sharpened. During copper-to-fiber work at an FAA remote radio site in **El Dorado, Arkansas** (with a secondary issue tied to **Amarillo, Texas**), a newly installed **Juniper Networks** router (Juniper is owned by HPE) replacing old Cisco equipment flooded FTI at roughly **1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds**. Newer IP switches stayed up. The **1980s-era TDM** circuits and older edge routers did not, and radar and voice feeds became unreliable across **17 of the 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers (ARTCCs)**. That forced a stop across all 22.

On **November 24**, CNN and CBS58 reported that investigators from the **FAA, FBI, Juniper, and Cisco** had **recreated the failure in a lab**. The cause was an **overlooked setting** on the new router that conflicted with the legacy routers the FAA is retiring. Investigators said they were **"90% sure"** it was an accident. The FAA has paused new installs of similar routers and kept the broadcast feature disabled on **roughly 60** deployed units while it waits on software patches.

<Figure src={networkImpedanceImg} alt="Architectural diagram of Network Impedance Mismatch showing a Modern Router (HPE-Juniper IP Core) saturating a legacy TDM Multiplexer's shallow queue buffer with 100 percent packet drop rate and frozen voice and radar telemetry">
  Figure 3: The El Dorado impedance mismatch — a high-throughput HPE/Juniper IP core router sending roughly 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds into shallow legacy TDM multiplexer buffers.
</Figure>

Two accountability threads matter more than the router itself:
- **Isolation speed:** Secretary Duffy criticized L3Harris for failing to quickly isolate the router's IP address during the event.
- **Oversight:** Air traffic and technician unions say a **2025 policy change** removed over-the-shoulder FAA oversight of contractor maintenance, so high-risk work no longer gets timing reviews or coordination with local FAA staff.

A missed setting is a human error. A missed setting that can reach 17 en-route centers, during the afternoon, with no FAA eyes on the change and no fast way to cut it off, is a **systems** failure. "Get used to it" excuses the second by pointing at the first.

## 3. Fault Domains, the $875 Million SMART Distraction, and Bedford's Warning

Three days before the ground stop, on **November 10**, the FAA expanded **SMART**, the **$875 million**, 12-year AI planning tool from **Air Space Intelligence**, to all **22 ARTCCs**, just **50 days** into a roughly **90-day** testing phase. That drew warnings from over 20 members of Congress and the **National Air Traffic Controllers Association (NATCA)**.

## 4. Microsoft's Lab Was Smaller Than Production

Microsoft's preliminary post-incident review for **`EX1550543`** (Exchange Online) and **`MO1550821`** (the wider Microsoft 365 suite) describes three factors that combined. The outage hit **430 million users** across 74+ countries, including the U.S. House, UK NHS hospitals, and Canadian "code grey" hospitals, over a 16-hour primary window.

1. **A latent `July 2026` defect:** A Windows Server update for M365 networking gateways introduced severe **lock contention** in a core secure-connection process. Testing missed it because the **lab ran lower-core hardware**; higher-core production servers jammed under load.
2. **A Teams failover ~15 hours earlier** moved traffic onto the primary gateway shared with Exchange Online.
3. **~14% of gateway capacity was already offline** for unrelated maintenance. Failing health checks pulled more nodes, the survivors drowned in retries, and **more than 80%** of gateway servers stopped responding.

<Figure src={retryLoopParalysisImg} alt="Four-stage cycle diagram titled Retry-Loop Paralysis: 1. Trigger (Peak Load + 14% Loss), 2. Drop (Health Checks Fail), 3. Saturation (Global Load Shifted), and 4. The Trap (Reboot Crushed by Retries)">
  Figure 8: How a latent July 2026 lock-contention bug plus ~14% offline capacity turned into global Retry-Loop Paralysis across >80% of M365 networking gateways.
</Figure>

The lesson is "your test environment has to look like production." That rule predates superintelligence by decades.

## 5. Contagion and Patches: Chase, United, NSANet, Apple

- **JPMorgan Chase (Nov 16):** A software bug in on-premise hardware disrupted web, mobile, ATM, and branch systems for about **8 hours**, peaking near **5,000** Downdetector reports. Because Chase sits in the middle of the rails, transfers stalled through **Zelle** (whose own network stayed up) and **Plaid**, and customers at **Bank of America, Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank, Capital One, and Truist** saw errors even though those banks' core systems were fine.
- **United Airlines (Nov 16):** A web-based weight-and-balance tool failed and halted mainline departures for about **90–100 minutes**.
- **NSA `NSANet` (Nov 17):** A routine maintenance patch for a PHP vulnerability failed to come back cleanly. The NSA declared **"code yellow"** at **Fort Meade** and staggered non-emergency staff arrivals until noon, in effect throttling login load by hand, before returning to "code green" that evening.
- **Apple (Nov 17):** An internal software update briefly took down iCloud sign-in, the App Store, Apple Music, Apple Pay, AppleCare, device activation, and retail point-of-sale.

## 6. "Get Used to It" vs. What Wang Actually Wrote

The White House response hasn't come with technical detail: individual events, companies upgrading, "bad things happen," and "ancient" federal infrastructure. National Cyber Director **Sean Cairncross** is out after Trump said he wasn't "good enough," and DoD CIO **Kirsten Davies** is the new cyber chief. On **November 18**, DNI Jay Clayton, DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, and FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia told the House Homeland Security Committee there was no evidence of a coordinated attack. The detailed causes came out through reporting, not briefings.

Wang's essay, published **November 22** and viewed **22.7 million** times by Nov 25, is being cited as proof that this is the new normal. Here is what it actually says:

> *"Superintelligence is arriving. How humanity acts over the next year will have great consequence on what future world we live in."*

> *"...we have seen coding be nearly solved, the emergence of extremely powerful cyber capabilities, and strong agentic capabilities which have enabled the models to become general-purpose useful aides."*

> *"I notice a particular form of lazy thinking within the AI industry, which is that ASI is inevitable and the only problem to solve is how to do it safely, but there is much more at stake."*

Wang builds the essay on what he calls "the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis." That is his claim, and I haven't verified it. More to the point, **the essay never mentions service outages or patch cycles.** The idea that we're in for a year or more of finding vulnerabilities, patching, and defending comes from the wider "Patchpocalypse" coverage. Tech Policy Press reports that publicly disclosed vulnerabilities passed **100,000** in mid-November, and Ivanti's "Patch Apocalypse" paper describes discovery running at machine speed while remediation stays at human speed.

That pressure is real. But "more vulnerabilities" and "more outages" are not the same thing. Patches are incoming work; outages happen when the process that deploys them fails. If discovery doubles, staged rollouts, production-scale testing, fault domains, rollback, and human oversight of high-risk changes have to scale with it. *(For the full deep dive on how machine-speed vulnerability discovery collides with human-speed change control, read the companion explainer: [The Patchpocalypse and the Great Tech Cascade](/2026/11/25/the-patchpocalypse-and-the-great-tech-cascade/).)* Read closely, Wang argues against the "get used to it" mindset: he calls inevitability thinking *lazy* and says safety, alignment, and security are "extremely urgent."

## 7. Questions for December 2, and the $50 Billion Ask

Secretary Duffy wants **$50 billion** for air traffic control modernization (up from **$30.1 billion**), arguing that **$15 billion** over 25 years bought only patchwork. Democratic appropriators point to the **$22.6 billion** already in the **FY2026 THUD** bill. Money buys hardware. It doesn't buy change discipline unless Congress requires it.

What I'd want Senators to ask Duffy and Bedford on **Dec 2**:
1. **What exactly was the overlooked El Dorado setting**, who approved the change window, and was it scheduled off-peak?
2. **Why did isolating the router's IP take so long**, and what's the runbook now?
3. **Did the 2025 policy remove FAA oversight of contractor maintenance?** Will it be restored?
4. **Why could one remote site destabilize 17 of 22 ARTCCs?** What segmentation and rate-limiting come next?
5. **What boundary and fault-injection tests gate the FENS cutover** before 2028?
6. **What metrics justified expanding SMART at 50 of 90 test days?**
7. **How much of the $50 billion is tied to change-safety controls**, not just hardware?
